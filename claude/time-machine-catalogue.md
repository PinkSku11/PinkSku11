# The Time Machine Catalogue — thirty-nine more devices, honestly labeled

*Time Travel project · design note, 2 October 2026, written in answer to "give me more time travel device ideas!" Sibling of the Greylock Time Machine (claude/greylock-time-machine.md) and Starship Time Machines (claude/starship-time-machines.md). Method: six independent idea-generation lenses ran in parallel (rotating labs, deep Earth, analog horizons, quantum devices, past-viewers, space missions), an adversarial checker re-derived every number in every entry before it could stay, and a completeness critic swept the merged set for gaps. 36 entries survived, 0 were killed outright, three independent submissions of the same famous wrong device were merged into one, and several numbers below carry the checker's corrections rather than the original claims. Status: catalogue only.*

## 0. The taxonomy

Every device below carries one letter, per the project's honesty rule:

- **A** — true proper-time machine: a real rate difference between the device's contents and a reference, readable with clocks.
- **B** — past-viewer / past-recorder: lets you observe or re-observe the past; moves nothing.
- **C** — subjective-time machine: biology or information skips *experienced* time; proper time unaffected.
- **D** — simulator / analog: runs the mathematics of horizons or time loops; moves nothing.
- **E** — forbidden: requires exotic matter or violates known physics; included only with the no-go.
- **F** — famous-but-wrong: a named, publicized device claim that fails; included only with the refutation.

A reminder from the Greylock note that governs everything here: forward is continuous (every m/s and every metre buys some future); backward is all-or-nothing, and the "all" is quantum-forbidden.

## 1. Spin machines — special relativity in a room

**1. The Mössbauer rotor, rebuilt (A, institutional, ~$50–150k).** A turbine rotor with a Co-57 gamma source on the axis and an Fe-57 resonant absorber on the rim at 500 rev/s: rim speed 208.6 m/s, slowdown v²/2c² = 2.42 × 10⁻¹³ = **20.9 ns/day** — twice Greylock, on a bench. The 14.4 keV Mössbauer line (Q ≈ 3.1 × 10¹²) is the clock; the 2.42 × 10⁻¹³ detuning is 0.75 of a natural linewidth, read as a count-rate change. The rim pulls 6.7 × 10⁴ g, which is the whole design lesson: the only clock that survives is a nucleus locked in a crystal lattice. Precedent: Hay, Schiffer, Cranshaw & Egelstaff, PRL 4, 165 (1960); Kündig, Phys. Rev. 129, 2371 (1963); Champeney–Isaak–Khan, Nature 198, 1186 (1963).

**2. The specific-strength flywheel (A, institutional, ~$1–5M).** For a thin rim, hoop stress σ = ρv², so the maximum dilation of *any* material centrifuge is **y_max = σ/(2ρc²)** — set purely by specific strength, independent of radius. Maraging steel: 120 ns/day. Wound carbon fiber at 4 GPa: 1,606 m/s rim, **1.24 µs/day**. Ideal graphene: 7,573 m/s, 27.6 µs/day — the absolute ceiling for matter rotors. A desk-sized machine that displays the materials-science speed limit of centrifugal time travel; clock = Mössbauer foil or NV spins bonded into the rim (2.6 × 10⁶ g at r = 10 cm).

**3. The muon storage ring (A, existing facility).** The Brookhaven/Fermilab g−2 ring at magic momentum 3.094 GeV/c, γ = 29.30: each stored muon experiences 2.2 µs while the lab ages 64.4 µs — a ferry that ships its contents **62 µs into the lab's future per lifetime**, under a proper acceleration of 1.1 × 10¹⁸ g, where the clock hypothesis (rate depends on v, not a) is verified. Bailey et al., Nature 268, 301 (1977); Fermilab g−2, PRL 126, 141801 (2021). The magnet was barged from Brookhaven to Fermilab in 2013 rather than rebuilt.

**4. The walking-pace time machine (A, institutional).** A trapped Al-27⁺ optical clock deliberately driven into harmonic motion and compared with its stationary twin. At 10 m/s rms the moving ion loses 0.048 ns/day; the demonstrated 8.6 × 10⁻¹⁸ clock inaccuracy resolves time dilation down to **1.24 m/s — literal walking pace**. Already done: Chou, Hume, Rosenband & Wineland, Science 329, 1630 (2010). The smallest object ever used as a relativistic traveler: one atom.

**5. The one-electron cyclotron — time dilation quantized (A, desktop-scale apparatus in a cryo lab, ~$1M).** A single electron in a 5.4 T Penning trap: relativity makes the 150 GHz cyclotron ladder anharmonic by exactly hν_c/mc² = 1.21 × 10⁻⁹ per quantum, read out via QND quantum jumps. Add one microwave photon, watch the clock rate step down by one discrete unit of slowdown — **the only machine where time dilation arrives in integer clicks**. Peil & Gabrielse, PRL 83, 1287 (1999).

## 2. Depth machines — the sign-flipped lens (going down makes you younger)

The sign lesson, stated once: clock rate follows the potential Φ, not g. Below ground g can even rise, but Φ keeps falling, so deeper is always slower.

**6. The 4850-Level Time Cellar (A, institutional, ~$3–6M).** A transportable Sr lattice clock on the 4850-ft level of SURF (the former Homestake mine, Lead SD), fiber-linked up the Yates shaft: 1,478 m of depth → **13.9 ns/day slow**, 5.1 µs/yr — the mirror twin of the Greylock summit station. Paired with the Ooty summit clock the project's total split becomes 20.0 ns/day. Precedent for underground transportable clocks: Grotti et al., Nature Physics 14, 437 (2018, Modane).

**7. The Hadal Time Capsule (A, institutional, ~$3–5M).** A benthic lander with a DSAC-class mercury-ion clock in a titanium sphere on the Challenger Deep floor (10,935 m — and the ocean surface *is* the geoid, so the full depth counts): **103.2 ns/day slow**, 0.72 µs per week on the bottom. The checker's clock budget is part of the design: the signal is 4,000× DSAC drift, while a $5k CSAC accumulates 250× the signal in its own aging — hopeless. Heritage: DSAC flew (Burt et al., Nature 595, 43, 2021); Five Deeps landers routinely dive hadal.

**8. The Borehole Chronometer Sonde (A, institutional, ~$2–5M).** Relativistic well-logging: a high-temperature clock sonde on wireline reads geopotential *directly* — what borehole gravimetry can only get by integrating. A 3-km geothermal well: 28.3 ns/day. KTB (9,101 m): 85.8 ns/day. Kola Superdeep (12,262 m): **115.7 ns/day, the largest sign-flipped rate anywhere in Earth's crust** — beating even Challenger Deep. The engineering wall is 180–260 °C bottom-hole temperature.

**9. The Three Gorges Spillway Time Knob (A, institutional, ~$5–10M).** An optical clock on a moored barge in a reservoir drawn down 30 m on schedule every year: the spillway gate becomes a time-dilation actuator, swinging the barge clock by 0.283 ns/day and banking ~52 ns per six-month low pool — the catalogue's only A-machine with a civil-engineering on-switch. Subtler second channel (order of magnitude): the 22 km³ of released water shifts a fixed dam-gallery clock by ~1.8 × 10⁻¹⁹ — unmeasurable today, a clean first-light target for the Th-229 nuclear-clock generation.

**10. The Lunar Rate Gauge (A, campaign on existing clocks, ~$1–2M).** Two fiber-linked lattice clocks on an intercontinental baseline run as a *tide instrument*: the Moon periodically re-tilts the geopotential, and the relative clock rate wobbles at the M2 period with net factor (1 + k₂ − h₂) = 0.69, peak ~1.5 × 10⁻¹⁷, integrated proper-time oscillation ~0.11 ps. Today tides are a correction in clock comparisons; this flips them into the signal — the Moon read out in ns/day instead of meters of sea level. Apparently still unclaimed as a dedicated detection.

**11. Feynman's Younger Core (F — the famous wrong number, with the limit case).** Feynman told Caltech students the Earth's center should be "a day or two" younger than the surface; textbooks repeated it for 50 years. The real answer (PREM density): **47.4 µs/day, an aging deficit of 2.5 years over Earth's 4.54 Gyr — Feynman was short by a factor of ~600**. Refutation: Uggerhøj, Mikkelsen & Faye, "The young centre of the Earth," Eur. J. Phys. 37, 035602 (2016). Unbuildable at 360 GPa and 5,500 K, but it is the limit case every depth machine above approximates.

## 3. Analog machines — horizons and time-edits for light, sound, and water

**12. The Soliton Horizon Fiber Bench (D, institutional, ~$150–400k).** An intense soliton in photonic-crystal fiber drags a moving refractive-index front a chasing probe pulse can never cross: a genuine white-hole group-velocity horizon, with analog Hawking temperature 17 mK (10 µm front) to 0.17 K (1 µm) — millions of times hotter than any astrophysical black hole, though spontaneous pairs remain below detection. Horizon measured: Philbin et al., Science 319, 1367 (2008); stimulated Hawking radiation: Drori et al., PRL 122, 010404 (2019).

**13. The BEC Waterfall Hawking Cavity (D, institutional, ~$0.5–1M).** A Rb-87 condensate swept supersonic forms a sonic horizon; density-correlation imaging shows entangled phonon pairs with a thermal spectrum at T_H = 0.35 nK — **the only laboratory measurement of spontaneous, thermal Hawking radiation of any kind**, equivalent in temperature to a 176 M☉ black hole. The lab beats astronomy because 0.35 nK is resolvable in a condensate while a real hole's 62 nK drowns under the 2.7 K CMB. Steinhauer, Nat. Phys. 12, 959 (2016); de Nova et al., Nature 569, 688 (2019).

**14. The Talbot History Editor (D, institutional, ~$100–300k).** A fiber temporal cloak: time lenses open dark "time holes" in a probe beam at 12.7 GHz, 46% duty, and stitch the beam seamlessly shut — any event inside a hole is never recorded downstream. Run continuously it erases **11 hours per day from one light beam's record of history**. The honesty line is built in: it hides events from that one probe field only; every other channel records normally. Fridman et al., Nature 481, 62 (2012); Lukens, Leaird & Weiner, Nature 498, 205 (2013).

**15. The Instant Time Mirror Tank (D, desktop, ~$10–30k).** A shallow water tank given a single 3-ms, 20-g vertical jolt: the sudden change acts as a time boundary for the whole 2D wavefield, and every ripple splits into a transmitted wave plus a time-reversed twin that runs the movie backward — a spreading splash refocuses into the shape of its source. Bacot et al., Nature Physics 12, 972 (2016); array cousins: Fink's time-reversal mirrors, Lerosey et al., Science 315, 1120 (2007).

**16. The Time-Boundary Echo Line (D, desktop, ~$3–10k — the cheapest working device in the catalogue).** A varactor-loaded coax line whose capacitance is snapped C → C/2 everywhere at once: a microwave pulse partially *reflects off the instant*, sending a time-reversed, frequency-shifted (×1.414) echo back to the source carrying 4.3% of the energy (energy-normalized Fresnel coefficients R = 0.207, T = 1.207). Time boundaries don't conserve wave energy — the switch bank pays the extra 50%. Moussa et al., Nature Physics 19 (2023); theory: Morgenthaler 1958.

## 4. Quantum machines — superposed time, reordered time, emergent time

**17. The Superposed Clock Tower (A — in superposition; institutional, ~$2–5M; NOT YET DONE).** The catalogue's flagship unbuilt experiment: one Sr atom, clock transition ticking, placed in spatial superposition at two heights in a 10-m fountain. The two branches accumulate *different proper time*, read out as interference-visibility loss V = |cos(ωΔτ/2)|. Full visibility null needs 10.67 m × 1 s (just beyond a 10-m tower — so the honest target is the 1 m / 1 s case: a 1.1% visibility drop); branch proper times then differ by 1.165 fs. **The only machine on any of these lists where a single object experiences two different amounts of elapsed time at once.** Proposal: Zych, Costa, Pikovski & Brukner, Nat. Commun. 2, 505 (2011); magnetic-gradient analog done: Margalit et al., Science 349, 1205 (2015); gravitational version still open.

**18. The After-the-Fact Entangler (D, institutional, ~$300–500k).** Delayed-choice entanglement swapping: photons 1 and 4 are detected and *gone*; 503 ns later (104 m of fiber) a quantum random number decides whether their surviving partners get Bell-measured — which decides whether the already-recorded events were entangled or separable. Sorted on that later choice, the past data violate Bell (measured S = 2.29 ± 0.09); unsorted, nothing retro ever shows (no-signaling: exactly 0 bits travel backward). Ma et al., Nature Physics 8, 479 (2012).

**19. The Past-Eraser Box (F, desktop, ~$5–25k).** The delayed-choice quantum eraser as a teaching kit, included to kill its own legend. The pop claim — a later choice reaches back and decides whether the earlier photon "was" wave or particle — fails by arithmetic the kit makes visible: the two "erased" coincidence patterns are fringes shifted by π whose sum reconstructs the fringe-free raw record at D0, which never changes. Experiment: Kim et al., PRL 84, 1 (2000); refutations: Kastner, Found. Phys. 49, 717 (2019); Fankhauser, arXiv:1707.07884.

**20. The Order-of-Events Blender (D, institutional, ~$150–400k).** The photonic quantum switch: a control qubit puts the *order* of two operations into superposition — A-then-B and B-then-A simultaneously, certified by a causal witness violated at ~7σ. Provable advantage: deciding whether two gates commute takes the switch 2 queries where any fixed-order circuit needs ≥3 (and n vs O(n²) in general). The closest working hardware to indefinite time-ordering; proper time untouched. Procopio et al., Nat. Commun. 6, 7913 (2015); Rubino et al., Sci. Adv. 3, e1602589 (2017).

**21. The Timeless Snow Globe (D, institutional, ~$50–150k).** The Page–Wootters mechanism on a bench: a two-photon "universe" whose global state is verifiably static — from outside, nothing ever happens — while an observer conditioning on the internal clock photon watches full dynamical evolution (one Bloch rotation per 90 µm of quartz clockwork). Time not fed into the box, but emerging from entanglement inside it. Moreva et al., PRA 89, 052122 (2014); Page & Wootters, PRD 27, 2885 (1983).

**22. The Grandfather-Paradox Referee (D, institutional, ~$150–300k).** One gate-teleportation rig, two rival time-travel theories, head-to-head on the identical grandfather circuit: Deutsch's fixed point dissolves the paradox into a coin flip (p = 0.5, entropy exactly 1 bit); Lloyd's P-CTC jams the gun (p = 0). Discriminator: distinguishing nonorthogonal states, where the Helstrom bound is 69.1% but a real Deutsch box scores 100% (Brun–Harrington–Wilde, PRL 102, 210402, 2009). If CTCs existed, an experiment could tell whose physics they obey; the simulator pays for its answers in postselection throughput, which is the no-go made tangible. Extends the project's existing P-CTC entry; the side-by-side referee run has never been published.

## 5. Viewing machines — instruments that genuinely show the past

**23. The Scheduled-Rewind Lens Monitor (B, institutional, ~$1–2M or free on Rubin/LSST alerts).** A robotic 1-m telescope on a multiply-imaged lensed source, printing the calendar date on which an observed flare will *replay* in the trailing image. This has worked: SN Refsdal's reappearance was predicted and then observed on 11 Dec 2015, 376.0 days after the Einstein-cross images — a 9.5-billion-year-old explosion watched twice, a year apart. Standing channel: RX J1131−1231 re-airs every quasar flicker ~91 days later. Kelly et al., ApJL 819, L8 (2016); delays: Kelly et al. 2023; Tewes et al., A&A 556, A22 (2013).

**24. The Light-Echo Spectrograph (B, institutional, survey-scale ~$1M).** Difference-image Galactic dust for moving echo arcs — supernova light arriving centuries late off interstellar dust sheets — and feed them to an 8-m spectrograph. This is how Tycho Brahe's supernova of 1572 was spectroscopically classified (Type Ia) **436 years after it exploded**, and Cas A (~1680) got its spectrum in 2008; different dust sheets replay the same explosion from different angles. The echo rings sweep the sky at an apparent 2.1c — superluminal-looking, perfectly causal. Krause et al., Nature 456, 617 (2008) and Science 320, 1195 (2008).

**25. The Lookback Dial (B, desktop, ~$750).** A 20-cm Dobsonian with an encoder whose display shows not the object's name but the date its light left: Moon 1.28 s ago; Sirius 2017; Polaris ~1593 (±a century of distance uncertainty); M13 ~20,000 BC; M31 2.5 million years ago; and 3C 273 at 2.04 Gyr — the deepest past a human eye can verify through an eyepiece, photons older than the oxygen they land in. The cheapest genuine past-viewer that can be built.

**26. The Two-Age Sun Comparator (B, detector-scale).** One star, two epochs, side by side: pp neutrinos exit the core unimpeded (2.3 s out, 499 s to Earth — the Sun *now*) while sunlight carries energy that random-walked out of the core over ~170,000 years (Mitalas & Sills 1992). Borexino actually ran the comparison — measured pp flux (6.6 ± 0.7) × 10¹⁰ /cm²/s, neutrino and photon luminosities agreeing at ~10% — certifying the Sun's core burned at today's rate across the whole photon diffusion time. Caveat kept honest: the photon channel records luminosity history, not an image. Borexino, Nature 512, 383 (2014).

**27. The Ice-Core Tape Deck (B, institutional).** A continuous-flow melter as a playback head: EPICA Dome C holds 800,000 years in 3,190 m (2.51 yr/cm), so a 3.5 cm/min melt streams climate at 8.8 years per minute — 350 yr/min in the thinned basal ice — and replaying the whole record takes 63 days of continuous melting. Readout software must subtract the 2–5.5 kyr gas-age/ice-age offset. Beyond EPICA reached bedrock in Jan 2025, extending the reel toward 1.2 Myr. EPICA, Nature 429, 623 (2004).

**28. The Vatican Chronovisor (F).** The most famous past-viewer claim — Fr. Pellegrino Ernetti's 1960s–70s screen that allegedly displayed any historical event, Crucifixion photo included (1972). The refutation is a flux integral: light from 33 CE is a shell 1,993 ly out; even granting a 100-m mirror pre-positioned at 996 ly, a 1-kW sunlit scene delivers one photon per 1.4 million years, and the return leg to Earth makes it one per 2 × 10⁴¹ years. Real past-viewers (entries 23–27) work only because their information-bearing photons are still in flight toward us.

## 6. Mission machines — five spacecraft that are time machines by design

**29. The Hermean Slow Clock (A, flagship-class ~$1.5–2B).** An optical-clock lander on Mercury: deep in the Sun's well, riding 47.9 km/s rms orbital speed, it loses **1.97 ms/day (0.72 s/year)** against Earth — the slowest-ticking place a clock can be landed with chemical propulsion — and Mercury's eccentricity sweeps the rate through a 12.7 ms waveform every 88 days, ~4.5 orders of magnitude louder than the Galileo 5/6 signal.

**30. The Molniya Relativity Waveform Generator (A, smallsat ~$50–150M).** A good clock in a 600 × 39,750 km orbit (e = 0.737) is a signal generator whose output waveform is general relativity: a 1.69 µs periodic timing tone every 12 h — 4.4× the amplitude of the accidental Galileo 5/6 test — demodulable by any university ground station, on top of a GPS-like +38.6 µs/day secular gain. The checker's caveat stands: a bare CSAC barely resolves the 9 × 10⁻¹⁰ peak modulation; the clock quality is the mission.

**31. The L2 Twin-Clock Station (A, Explorer-class ~$300–500M).** Build two identical optical lattice clocks; one stays in the lab basement, its twin flies to Sun–Earth L2 with a coherent optical link home. The traveling twin **gains 59.7 µs/day and finishes a 5-year mission 0.109 s older than its sibling** — macroscopic, human-readable differential aging, with the sign *inverted* from the pop twin paradox because escaping Earth's potential beats the extra orbital speed. Flight precedent: ACES/PHARAO to the ISS, April 2025.

**32. The Solar-Gravitational-Lens Past-Viewer (B, ~$2–3B, multi-decade).** Fly a 1-m telescope past 547.8 AU on the Sun's focal line and the Sun becomes a lens with on-axis amplification 1.17 × 10¹¹, resolving ~25 km/pixel on an exoplanet at 30 pc — whose light is 98 years old on arrival. The only buildable device that delivers megapixel footage of another world's previous century. Turyshev & Toth, NIAC Phase III (arXiv:2002.11871).

**33. The Escape-Velocity Fast Clock (A, ~$1.8B).** An optical clock on an Interstellar-Probe-class solar-escape trajectory: the fastest-aging clock reachable with real propulsion, the exact mirror of the Mercury lander. Corrected by the adversarial check (the original claim forgot that the hyperbolic excess speed never goes away): at v∞ = 7 AU/yr the permanent kinetic term costs 6.1 × 10⁻⁹, leaving an asymptotic gain of **+0.81 ms/day (~21× GPS) and ~15 seconds banked ahead of Earth over a 50-year mission** — not the naive +1.34 ms/day and 24.5 s. Slower exits keep more of the gravitational gain.

## 7. The merged debunk: Mallett's ring-laser time machine (F)

Submitted independently by three of the six lenses — a measure of its fame. Ronald Mallett's claim (Phys. Lett. A 269, 214, 2000; Found. Phys. 33, 1307, 2003; the 2006 book *Time Traveler*): circulating laser light frame-drags spacetime into closed timelike curves. Three refutation layers, all verified: (i) magnitude — 1 MW circulating in a 1-m ring drags frames at ~10⁻³⁷ rad/s, one radian per 2.2 × 10¹⁹ ages of the universe; a cavity-enhanced tabletop version does no better than 1.1 × 10¹⁹ universe ages; (ii) scaling — CTC formation needs the weak field to break down, E ~ c⁴R/G ≈ 36 Earth masses of pure light energy in a 16-cm ring; (iii) structure — Olum & Everett, Found. Phys. Lett. 18, 379 (2005) showed the CTCs in Mallett's exact solution are supplied by a pre-existing line singularity (a cosmic-string-like defect), not by the laser at all, with Hawking's chronology theorem (PRD 46, 603, 1992) closing the singularity-free version.

## 8. The critic's six — gaps the catalogue still had

1. **Torpor Transit Pod (C)** — a medically induced torpor bay modeled on the arctic ground squirrel (core to −2.9 °C, metabolism ~2% of basal, ~8 months of experienced time skipped per winter): the first entry in the otherwise empty C category, and the honest frame for every hibernation/cryonics claim — proper time wholly unaffected.
2. **Moonbounce Echo Station (B)** — a ham-radio EME rig: your own voice back from **2.56 seconds in the past** (768,800 km round trip at c); the cheapest self-referential past re-observer, often free at a club station.
3. **Deep-Time Genome Reader (B)** — ancient environmental DNA as a playback head: 2-million-year-old eDNA from Greenland's Kap København Formation reconstructed a mastodon-bearing Arctic ecosystem no instrument witnessed (Kjær et al., Nature 2022).
4. **Binary-Pulsar Einstein-Delay Recorder (A)** — nature supplies the relativistic clock: PSR B1913+16 runs measurably slow and fast around its 7.75-h eccentric orbit with a 4.295 ms Einstein-delay amplitude, read against terrestrial clocks.
5. **Ori Vacuum-Core Time Machine (E)** — the one serious "no exotic matter needed" CTC claim (Ori, PRL 2005); closed honestly: it requires a measure-zero, unstable set of initial data, and vacuum stress-energy still diverges on the would-be chronology horizon.
6. **Philadelphia Experiment / USS Eldridge (F)** — the 1943 "ship displaced in time" legend fails against the ship's own deck logs (she was in New York Harbor) and traces entirely to Carl Allen's 1955 hoax letters, per the Office of Naval Research.

## 9. What to build first (ranked by signal per dollar)

1. **Time-Boundary Echo Line** (~$3–10k): a working reflection-off-an-instant on a hobbyist budget — the project's best new winter build alongside the CosmicWatch muon pair.
2. **Lookback Dial** (~$750): turns the existing telescope idea into a device with a date readout; pairs with the Greylock exhibit.
3. **Instant Time Mirror Tank** (~$10–30k): the most visually arresting demo in the catalogue (a splash un-splashing).
4. **Past-Eraser Box** (~$5–25k): buildable from a commercial entangled-photon teaching kit, and it debunks the most-viewed time-travel myth on the internet using its own raw data.
5. **The Superposed Clock Tower** (§4.17) is the flagship ambition: the one experiment on this list that is both undone and decisive — a single atom experiencing two elapsed times at once. It belongs in the same letter campaign as the Greylock clock loan and the Greylock-1 cubesat.

## 10. Method and sources

Generated by six parallel lenses with an adversarial verifier per lens (every number above re-derived independently; corrections applied include: SN Refsdal's earlier image is late-1990s, not 1964; Borexino's 6.6 × 10¹⁰ is the measured flux; the escape-clock gain reduced ~35% for the permanent v∞ term; the Mercury waveform is ~4.5 orders over Galileo, not 6; the fiber-horizon temperature beats stellar black holes by millions, not billions; a double-counted tidal displacement term removed) and a completeness critic (§8). Primary sources are cited inline per entry; the project's standing no-go ledger (Hawking 1992, Ford–Roman 1995, CFGO 1994, Olum–Everett 2005) is inherited from the sibling notes.
