## 1. Corrected

1. **Satellite Ledger eccentricity labels (factor 2).** 2√(GM·A)·e/c² at A = 26,560 km gives **amplitude** 22.9 ns at e = 0.01 and 45.8 ns at e = 0.02; the record's "23 ns peak-to-peak / 46 ns" mislabels amplitudes as p-p. True peak-to-peak: 45.8 and 91.6 ns. (The formula itself is the exact IS-GPS-200 term; the constant −4.4647 × 10⁻¹⁰, 38.58 µs/day, the factory frequency, 20,182 km and 3.874 km/s all confirm.)
2. **Muon Hourglass measures a station ratio, not a survival probability.** The "5%" is for a rounded 2 km (the named stations' 2,140 m gives 3.9%), and — the deeper point — single-muon survival is not what two CosmicWatch counters compare: muons are born over a range of altitudes, lose ~460 MeV of ionization between Ooty and Manipal (~230 g/cm² of air), and ride a steep spectrum, so the **real measured flux ratio is ~1.5–2×, not 20×**. The record's own code plan (Gaisser spectrum, decay Monte Carlo, barometric corrections) handles exactly this; the Physics line should say the ratio is the observable and the Monte Carlo is what turns it into a dilation test. γ ≈ 20 (2.1 GeV) is the low side of typical (sea-level mean ~4 GeV, γ ≈ 38); "most survive" still holds (85% at γ = 20).
3. **Flume temperature: 2.4 pK, not 2 pK** at κ = 2 s⁻¹ (T = ħκ/2πk_B; ~20% low; 2 pK would be κ = 1.65 s⁻¹). Mechanism and citations exactly right.
4. **The companion list is from another repository's reality.** Of the five claude/ files named, only greylock-time-machine.md exists here; ooty-clock-run-protocol.md, the-time-machine-that-runs-on-a-laptop.md, time-ships.md and chronology-horizon-01-setup.md do not — while the record ignores what this repo *does* hold (time-machine-catalogue.md, starship-time-machines.md, greylock-software.md + the greylock/ package, chrysalis-time-ships.xlsx with its second wing, timeship-audit.md, traveler-physiology.md).
5. **"Exists: timeship | qiskit bundle" — not in this repository.** Only greylock/ is checked in. The timeship and Qiskit code live in other sessions' chats; the standing recommendation of timeship-audit.md (commit timeship v0.1 next to greylock/) now applies to the Qiskit bundle too.
6. **ublox.py does not yet read ephemerides.** It decodes TIM-TP and NAV-PVT only; the Satellite Ledger's "log raw ephemerides and clock terms" needs an RXM-SFRBX + LNAV subframe decoder (bit-packed 300-bit subframes with ICD scale factors — a real 200–300-line job), or the lazier road: the existing raw-UBX tee + rinex.py convbin pipeline → parse RINEX nav files. "A weekend" is fair only on the second road. (The UbxParser framing class, raw tee and rinex.py all genuinely carry over.)
7. **Fizeau historical attribution.** The record's formula is a correct **single-pass Mach–Zehnder** variant (0.172 fringe; the classic double-pass geometry gives 0.34 — the record should state which arm geometry it means, since the ambiguity is exactly 2×), and (n²−1) ≡ n²(1−1/n²) is fully consistent with Fresnel drag. But "Michelson–Morley 1886 got 0.39" is a back-computation at HeNe wavelength, not their reported number (they measured at ~570 nm and quoted the result as a drag coefficient, 0.434 ± 0.02).
8. **Internal nit:** gyre.py is "three lines" in §3 and "20 lines" in §4.

Cross-document note: our time-machine-catalogue.md uses the 4π convention (1.04 × 10⁻³⁷, "2.2 × 10¹⁹ universe ages" — also internally consistent), and the gravitomagnetic-dipole derivation gives 8π. The spread is an honest O(2–4) model ambiguity (ring vs dipole, null-source pressure terms); every convention returns the same verdict.
- **Roman Ring: the algorithm was implemented and run.** Bellman–Ford on the three-Proxima-Lantern graph (throats ∓2.33 yr, space legs 4.25 yr): **no negative cycle — confirmed**, with the sharpenings in §0: the tightest cycle is exactly **zero** (out one throat forward, home through another backward, co-located transfers free), so the idealized system sits on the chronology margin; a single lag > 4.25 yr closes a CTC, as does **any lag asymmetry between co-located Lanterns** (lags 2.34/2.33 → cycle weight −0.01 yr — the wormhole version of differential aging), and a chained ring of individually subcritical wormholes across multiple sites does too (Visser's actual configuration, confirmed computationally).
